# Issue #68 — Add newly tried dishes to the household repertoire after an approved, completed week

## Goal

After a planning week has elapsed, promote eligible newly introduced dishes from that week's final plan into the household's established meal catalog. Promotion is based on the exact final version and the historical new-food policy that applied when it was planned. It must be repeat-safe and must not let a later profile-policy change rewrite the decision for an already-planned week.

The issue's rule is: consider an active plan after `week_end` when its final version was either revision-free or explicitly accepted. “Tried” is best-effort: an approved plan plus the elapsed week is sufficient evidence that its planned dishes were implemented. Partial weeks are not inferred as fully cooked from telemetry; after the elapsed-week gate, the plan's final grid is the candidate set, with explicitly rejected dishes removed.

## Current branch and remaining scope

PR #108 implements and verifies the evidence writes described below. The last approved iteration ended there. This follow-up delivers scheduled completion and catalog promotion in the same PR. The feature is complete when a later plan can use an eligible dish as familiar after the scheduler processes the prior week.

[GitHub issue #68](https://github.com/satwikhebbar/kipp/issues/68) defines approval as revision-free or accepted. An untouched original plan needs no tap. Show **Accept plan** only on revised versions. Remove **Reject plan** from new Telegram plan messages. Keep handling outstanding version-bound callbacks until their ordinary expiry and honor whole-version rejections already recorded. Keep **Give feedback** and the Mini App review link. Explicit dish rejection still comes through ordinary plan-wide feedback under the rules below.

## Current architecture and constraints

- `MealProfile.mealDefinitions` owns the durable household catalog. Definitions carry `established` or `provisional` status; `dishRepertoire` is deprecated.
- New definitions are stored as plan-local provisional definitions in immutable `meal_plan_version` snapshots. Revisions inherit previous provisional definitions even when a definition is no longer present in the grid (`src/meal-planning/hydration.ts`), so promotion must join against the final grid rather than promote the entire snapshot.
- A plan stores its current version and may be changed by a CAS-guarded revision. Creating another plan replaces the previous active plan regardless of its week. The replacement transaction now records an immutable `replaced_at`. Initial and revision workflow runs are bounded and finish after sending the plan; no existing live process remains to run at `week_end`.
- The household catalog is persisted in `meal_profile.profile_json`; the store has D1 and in-memory implementations. The store is the trust boundary for atomic checks and profile writes.
- Existing planner validation and catalog conversion in `src/meal-planning/catalog.ts` must remain authoritative. A completion job must not accept client-authored meal definitions.
- Durable workflow operations replay. Completion processing must be idempotent and must couple eligibility checks to the profile write so an approval change, revision, or concurrent profile write cannot produce an invalid promotion.

## Proposed design

### 1. Persist version-scoped approval and historical policy

Keep the existing forward-only persistence and deadline guards for the decision inputs:

- `allow_new_foods_snapshot`: the boolean profile policy used to evaluate that version. Populate it on initial plan creation and each successful revision from the exact profile/context used for that run. Do not read the current profile setting when processing a completed week.
- `approved_version`: a plan-level nullable version pointer (or equivalent explicit approval record). Initial version is approved when created, matching the existing “created plan is approved by default” behavior. A successful revision clears approval of the prior version and leaves the new version pending acceptance. An authenticated parent “Accept this version” action sets the pointer only if the submitted plan ID/version is still the active current version and the database clock is strictly before `week_end`. Explicit rejection of the whole version clears approval under the same cutoff. Stale or expired acceptance/rejection actions do nothing.
- `rejected_meal_ids`: durable, plan-scoped dish-level rejection evidence. For ordinary authenticated feedback, use direct, explicit rejection clauses and resolve their dish targets against normalized provisional names and aliases on the current plan. Persist canonical IDs before starting the revision, so they survive a no-change or failed revision. Preserve IDs through later revisions. Dish rejection excludes only the identified dish. Whole-version rejection clears approval for that version and makes it ineligible unless a later final version is accepted.

Approval/rejection state is frozen at the exclusive `week_end` cutoff: acceptance, whole-version rejection, and dish-rejection feedback count only if the authorizing database statement executes strictly before `week_end` and the transaction succeeds. Statements that would authorize a write at or after the cutoff are rejected, including a revision started before close whose version insert executes after close. A transaction authorized before cutoff can finish afterward. Its dependent writes use the authorizing statement’s timestamp. A version is eligible when it is the final current version authorized before cutoff and either no revision was ever made (version 1 remains approved by creation) or the current version was explicitly accepted before cutoff. Any revision invalidates the preceding version's approval. Whole-version rejection makes the version ineligible until a later final version is accepted. Dish-level rejection excludes only that dish; it does not invalidate version approval. Later versions do not erase prior dish-rejection evidence.

Legacy versions with no policy snapshot are ineligible for automatic promotion. This fail-closed behavior avoids applying today's policy retroactively. Existing rows with no approval metadata are likewise ineligible unless the store can prove their version-1 revision-free status from existing authoritative data and the issue explicitly permits that compatibility path.

#### Match the target of a rejection

Recognize a small set of explicit rejection phrases such as “remove”, “I reject”, and “I don’t like”, then match the complete dish name or alias in the target position. A conjunction followed by a new action ends the current target list. Prefer the longest complete match for overlapping names and skip a target if the same normalized name or alias maps to multiple dishes. Require a clear end to each target. Skip ingredient edits, negated requests to remove a dish, and clauses whose target or meaning is unclear. Ordinary feedback still reaches revision processing when no explicit rejection can be recorded.

| Feedback | Durable rejection |
| --- | --- |
| “Please remove poha and add idli” | Poha only |
| “Please remove onions from poha” or “Please remove poha’s onions” | None |
| “I do not want to remove poha” | None |
| “Please remove poha and idli” | Both, if both names resolve uniquely |
| “Please remove masala poha” when both poha and masala poha exist | Masala poha only |
| “Please remove breakfast bowl” when two dishes share that alias | None |

#### Decide cutoff and success inside the write

Use D1's SQL clock in the statement that authorizes each change. Compare against the persisted UTC `week_end` at matching precision and require strictly earlier time. Use the same database timestamp for the qualifying write and its evidence. Preflight queries, webhook timestamps, and timestamps captured before an awaited D1 request cannot authorize a write. The in-memory store samples its clock after any awaited work and immediately before synchronous mutation.

- **Initial creation and replacement.** Guard the first insert with the target week's cutoff, profile existence, and a valid generation lease. Insert a new plan row in a non-active state inside the atomic D1 batch, using a database timestamp. Use that newly inserted row as the success prerequisite for inserting version 1, replacing the previous active plan, activating and approving the new plan, updating the profile interaction generation, and consuming the lease. Replace the old plan before activating the new one to respect the one-active-plan index. Stamp the old plan's `replaced_at` with the same database timestamp that authorized creation. If the new week is closed, the batch must leave the prior plan, profile generation, and lease untouched. Surface the late result explicitly to the workflow and deliver no plan for that write. A valid next-week creation can replace an already closed prior plan.
- **Revision promotion.** In the first statement of one D1 batch, conditionally insert the successor version only if the plan is active, the expected base is still current, the profile exists, the database time is before cutoff, and any existing feedback batch to consume is still processing and belongs to that chat, plan, and base version. Every later mutation proves that the exact successor was inserted, including the pointer and approval changes, inventory, profile interaction generation, and feedback batch writes. Use the inserted version's timestamp for the dependent changes. Use the plan/version key, base version and feedback batch identity to tie effects to the successor. Preserve a plain insert with a uniqueness constraint so a duplicate cannot turn into apparent success. A zero-row insert leaves the existing version and all related state unchanged. A SQL error rolls back the whole batch.
- **Version decisions and dish rejection.** Guard the single version decision update by the database clock and current active version. For dish rejection, the read establishes the candidate IDs and previous JSON; the CAS write checks active plan, current version, prior JSON, and the database clock on every attempt. On a failed CAS, reread before retrying. An already recorded rejection is a no-op and may be acknowledged only when the target version is still current and open.

Make the proof of success specific to the current batch. For creation, gate the staging row and its consumers on a fresh operation identity or an equivalent transaction-local proof. For revision, gate the plan pointer update immediately after the version insert using the insert result within SQL, then gate the remaining effects on that transition in the same batch. Verify any use of SQLite `changes()` across batch statements in the D1 test harness. If it is unavailable there, use a persisted per-attempt identity to guard the dependent writes. A preexisting row with the same plan/version identity cannot authorize effects for an unsuccessful insert.

### 2. Run completion on a scheduled path and preserve eligible history

Add an hourly Worker cron at minute 15 (`15 * * * *`) in the development and production manifests. Route it from the Worker's scheduled handler to the meal planning completion processor using the existing D1 binding. The first successful sweep after the household's stored `week_end` can process the plan, normally within an hour of the local Saturday cutoff. Use the database clock to determine whether a plan is due. A missed sweep is recovered by later sweeps because selection includes overdue, unprocessed plans. The Telegram workflow can finish after publishing as it does today.

Select due, eligible rows in bounded pages ordered by `(week_end, plan_id)` and use a matching index. Advance past individual conflicts or failures within a sweep. Retry profile conflicts from a fresh read with a bounded retry count, then leave the row eligible for the next sweep. Record terminal outcomes for eligible plans with zero additions, including invalid or ambiguous dish mappings, so they cannot block future work. Use bounded work per invocation and verify that processing failures are visible and that a failure for one plan does not abort the rest of the page. Update `wrangler.toml` and `wrangler.prod.toml` under the production runtime configuration contract. The job needs no new runtime variables or secrets.

The completion query considers only the latest plan for each chat/week and requires durable evidence that it was active at `week_end`. Require `created_at < week_end` and either active status or immutable `replaced_at > week_end`. The creation guard also excludes any late version 1 already written before the new persistence guard. A different-week replacement before or exactly at `week_end` disqualifies the old plan; a replacement after `week_end` preserves eligibility. A newer plan for the same chat/week supersedes the old chain regardless of timestamps. Keep the completion marker on the plan (or a small durable completion ledger) so delayed scheduler runs can recover it.

The store reads the candidate plan/version evidence and the exact stored profile JSON, then prepares the merged catalog. In one D1 batch, conditionally insert a completion ledger row keyed by plan ID, with a fresh processing attempt ID, matched version, and compact counts. The insert rechecks the database clock, latest plan for the chat/week, active-at-cutoff evidence, current and approved version, historical policy, no existing completion, and equality of the exact profile JSON. Gate the profile update on the ledger row carrying that specific attempt ID in the same batch. A zero-row insert cannot authorize a write, even on replay. A batch failure rolls back both mutations. A successful zero-addition outcome records completion without rewriting the profile. Mirror those guarantees in the in-memory store.

Use exact prior JSON as the shared compare-and-set guard for writers that rebuild `meal_profile.profile_json` from a read. Verify the catalog utility's existing guard and add one to legacy normalization. If normalization loses a race, reread current data before returning or retrying. A profile compare-and-set conflict leaves no completion marker and processing retries from a fresh read.

A revision or same-week replacement racing completion changes the current/latest-version guard and makes the old completion attempt a no-op. Active-plan replacement writes `replaced_at` in the same transaction: replacement before the deadline disqualifies the old plan; replacement after the deadline preserves its frozen eligibility. A profile update through any existing writer participates in the shared CAS protocol, and completion retries from a fresh profile read. Replayed jobs observe the processed marker and do nothing. Delayed processing after `week_end` is allowed only when active-at-deadline evidence and all other frozen eligibility facts match.

### 3. Derive only dishes represented in the final grid

Use the final version's persisted grid as the source of planned dishes. Current cells store a canonical dish name, so match it to a unique provisional definition by normalized canonical name or alias. Use a stable definition ID if the persisted cell supplies one. Skip an ambiguous match, and treat familiar catalog cells as ordinary non-candidates. Promote only definitions referenced by at least one cell, deduplicate repeated cells, exclude explicitly rejected IDs, and skip IDs or normalized names or aliases already established in the current catalog. Deduplicate among additions as well. Never promote a provisional definition that was inherited from an earlier revision and dropped from the final grid.

The planning model supplies the new dish and its structured ingredients during plan generation or revision. Hydration validates the proposal and saves its ingredients as the provisional definition's required ingredients alongside principal ingredients, eligible slots, packing suitability, cook time, and preparation needs. At completion, validate the saved definition and change its status to established while preserving its identity and those attributes, including any saved optional ingredients or choices. Reuse suitable catalog validation without applying the parent-entered catalog helper's assumption that every dish is packable. Preserve an unpackable dish's packing restrictions. If a candidate definition is missing, malformed, or ambiguous, skip and log the outcome. Completion makes no model call and does not expand a dish name into an ingredient list. The next planner reads the established definitions through the normal profile load.

### 4. Report the result narrowly

The scheduled path has no synchronous parent request. Record a compact processed result (added names/count, skipped count, and reason category) sufficient for runtime diagnostics and retry investigation. Do not store provider prose or duplicate full definition payloads in a new log. A user-facing notification is out of scope unless issue acceptance explicitly requires it.

## Acceptance criteria for implementation

- No repertoire mutation occurs before the plan's `week_end`.
- A revision-free version 1 is eligible under the existing default-approved behavior; after any revision, only the explicitly accepted current final version is eligible. Approval, whole-version rejection, dish rejection, and revision authorization require execution of the authorizing database statement strictly before `week_end`. A revision that reaches its insert at or after cutoff is rejected.
- The policy snapshot, not the current household setting, determines eligibility. Missing historical policy evidence fails closed.
- Candidate dishes come from the approved final grid intersected with its provisional definitions; dropped definitions and explicitly rejected meal IDs are excluded.
- A different-week replacement before `week_end` disqualifies the replaced plan; one after `week_end` does not erase the plan that was active at the deadline. Same-week supersession excludes older chains. Revisions/replacements racing processing fail the expected-version/latest-plan/deadline checks.
- Delayed and replayed scheduler delivery is safe. Profile changes from the catalog utility, normalization path, and workflow are merged without overwriting concurrent preference, policy, or catalog changes through the shared version/exact-JSON CAS protocol.
- Promoted definitions become available to later plans, retain existing validation constraints, and do not mutate historical plan versions.
- D1 and in-memory behavior match. Tests cover policy changes, absent policy, initial versus revised approval, expired accept/reject actions, dish-level rejection versus whole-version rejection, feedback rejection surviving no-change/failed revisions, final-grid intersection, replacement before versus after `week_end`, same-week supersession, revision authorization at or after cutoff, profile writes through existing writers, races, delayed execution, and replay.
