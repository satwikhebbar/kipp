School-Day Meal Planning Workflow

A product contract for helping a parent turn household context and weekly realities into a practical, easy-to-adjust school-week meal plan.

This HTML edition mirrors the accompanying product specification. It defines behaviour and user outcomes, not the implementation architecture.

1. Goal and intended outcome

Help a parent turn the household’s dietary needs, available ingredients, and that week’s exceptions into an active, easy-to-scan school-week meal plan for a child. The plan must be practical to follow and easy to adjust without replanning the whole week.

On Sunday, the parent can start a planning conversation, provide exceptions and available vegetables, review a Monday–Saturday plan at a glance, and make a few targeted feedback requests. In the absence of pending feedback, the latest plan is active for that week.

During the week, the parent can reopen the plan, find a video for either lunch meal, or guide a minimally disruptive update.

2. Scope and user

In scope for v1

  • A configurable Monday–Saturday school-week plan and configurable daily meal slots. The initial household has breakfast, two school snacks, packed school lunch, and home lunch.
  • Profile- and conversation-based planning; spoken or typed weekly input; voice-friendly feedback; and a mobile-friendly weekly grid.
  • Vegetarian school meals as a workflow constant for the initial India-focused release.
  • YouTube-first video discovery for lunch meals and minimally disruptive updates.

Out of scope for v1

  • A home-day template, automatic learning from outcomes, grocery ordering, a dedicated native app, and refrigerator-photo inventory recognition.

User: the parent who plans, prepares, and packs the child’s food. The bot user controls configuration and the active plan. v1 has no roles, sharing model, or separate approval persona.

3. Planning context

A useful plan reflects the parent’s real decision environment. Every context item is a hard constraint, soft preference, operating limit, recurring target, or week-specific fact.

Child eating profile

Hard dietary exclusions, favourites and foods to avoid, sensory guidance, new-food tolerance, and school realities such as utensil comfort or food-sharing restrictions.

Household repertoire

Preferred cuisines and dish families, slot-specific familiar dishes, useful formats, wasteful or disliked formats, substitutions, and repeat tolerance.

School-day schedule

School days, holidays, half days, break durations, meal-slot purpose, packing requirements, and optional school no-go guidance.

Preparation capacity

The real before-school workload, meals normally cooked fresh, dry snack defaults, prior-night prep tolerance, equipment gaps, and packing constraints.

Nutrition and variety

Health defaults, a cheat day, optional household targets, weekly balance, and the desired variety of dishes and ingredients.

Ingredients and procurement

Stable pantry baseline, weekly perishables and urgency, shortages, a no-shopping default, quick-commerce assumptions, household city and country.

History and feedback

Recent active plans are first-order workflow records used for cross-week variety. Household repertoire is workflow-managed from active plans and later explicit learning. Meal feedback is transactional: it drives the present revision but does not become permanent context without an explicit promotion step.

4. Configuration and weekly state

Generic properties are concepts the platform knows in code, with defined semantics and shape. Custom properties are user-created natural-language policies or overrides that the planner interprets as planning data only—not executable instructions. For every relevant custom policy, the proposed plan records whether it is satisfied, carries a labelled trade-off, or needs clarification; it must not claim certainty when the policy cannot be interpreted confidently.

Weekly conversational capture

The parent never fills an internal form. A Telegram message or voice note is transcribed, preserved, and summarized for correction. The agent derives a patch to weeklyInventory and/or weeklyExceptions, retaining the source and timestamp. Low-risk, unambiguous facts are applied; consequential ambiguity earns one concise question.

weeklyInventory: items with name, availability, quantity/use note; free-text notes
weeklyExceptions: kind, optional day/meal slots, free-text instruction

These values are week-scoped conversation state. They expire with that plan and never become persistent household context unless the parent explicitly promotes them.

Initial custom policies

  • Snack policy: school snacks are usually dry, quick to pack, and not cooked that morning.
  • Equipment gap: for example, no microwave.
  • Packing capacity: for example, two compartments and no leak-prone items.
  • Nutrition targets: fruit three to four times weekly, plus regular nuts or dry fruits.

5. Planning policy

  • Never violate dietary, allergy, or vegetarian constraints. School policies are guidance: follow them where practical, otherwise label the least-bad feasible trade-off.
  • Favor available, perishable ingredients. Easy-buy additions must be few, clearly labelled, and judged as standard grocery items or seasonally available in the given city and country.
  • Apply health goals as best effort, with one intentional cheat day.
  • Keep packed foods dry by default. Snacks should be pre-prepared or quick, dry items; a substantial cooked snack must be genuinely quick and fit the morning budget.
  • Treat school lunch and home lunch as fresh cooked. Breakfast is preferably fresh when capacity permits. Assess the combined before-school workload, not each recipe alone.
  • Suggest no more than two meals requiring prior-night prep on any day.
  • Optimize across the whole week. Avoid repeated named dishes and normally cap a principal ingredient at two meals weekly, while respecting requested repeats.
  • Apply generic properties and measurable operating limits as rules. For a relevant custom policy, record a concise satisfied, trade-off, or needs-clarification outcome; consequential ambiguity or a conflict with a hard constraint earns one concise question.
  • Retain structured rationale for every cell. The grid shows compact labels for meaningful trade-offs, such as easy-buy additions or required prep.
Stored plan and meal data: the plan retains outcomes for relevant custom policies, including affected cells and rationale. Each cell retains its name and slot, preparation label, required/optional prep, principal ingredients, inventory use, easy-buy additions, lunch video result, and structured rationale/trade-off labels. The initial UI can reveal only what is useful at a glance.

6. Weekly planning flow

  1. On Sunday, the parent starts planning through Telegram.
  2. The agent asks only for missing, week-specific exceptions and inventory, using persistent context rather than re-asking it.
  3. The agent asks targeted clarification when it materially changes the plan, then proposes a complete weekly grid.
  4. The parent reviews the grid and gives natural-language feedback on a meal or whole day. This is guidance to the agent, not direct cell replacement.
  5. Feedback remains pending until the parent asks for an updated plan. The current plan stays visible with a pending-changes indicator.
  6. The agent processes feedback together and returns a complete revised grid, which immediately becomes active. A separate finalization step is unnecessary.

7. Active plan and review

Telegram is the conversational surface: triggering the plan, supplying weekly context, answering clarifications, and asking the agent to process feedback. The Mini App is the visual surface: reviewing the weekly grid, opening meal details, and recording feedback. They do not need to duplicate every action in v1.

8. Recipe-video behaviour

  • Attempt discovery ahead of time for school lunch and home lunch only.
  • Prefer configured YouTube chefs and channels; a reputable fallback is acceptable.
  • Suitability includes the meal, dietary rules, vegetarian policy, cuisine preferences, and school context.
  • If no suitable video is found, retain the meal and store an explicit no-video result. Do not substitute a generic popularity ranking.

9. Midweek changes

  • Existing accepted cells are implicitly approved. Make the smallest change that addresses the feedback and preserve the remaining week.
  • A comment such as “this whole day is untenable” deliberately widens scope. Broader optimization is allowed only when clearly justified by the user’s comments.
  • Return a full revised grid after batched feedback is processed, and identify consequential additions with plan labels.
  • “Tomorrow is a holiday” updates weekly state but does not itself revise the plan. The parent explicitly asks to recreate remaining days if desired.
  • If a requested change may conflict with a hard constraint, seek clarification in Telegram before revising.

10. Product quality and remaining decisions

The workflow must be configurable, safe with hard exclusions, low-effort, morning-feasible, varied, editable without erasing accepted work, and accessible throughout the week. The initial quality metric is the number of feedback comments attached to each plan that becomes active.

Still open: the persistent-profile editing experience; a detailed and implementable recipe-source ranking policy; the precise measure and explanation of morning capacity; refrigerator-photo input; and any broader shopping-support design.
